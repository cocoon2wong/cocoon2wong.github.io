### Overview of the Echolocation Trilogy

Recently, the last part of our *Echolocation Trilogy*, the journal paper entitled "Reverberation: Learning the Latencies Before Forecasting Trajectories", has been finally accepted by IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence.
As elaborated in the last *Authors' Note* section in its Appendix,

> This study continues the conceptual thread of our previous works SocialCircle (CVPR2024) and *Resonance* (ICCV2025), but it is not a direct extension of either of these papers.
> Rather, it follows the same philosophical path that views trajectory prediction as a scan-reflect-echo process, as illustrated in the first figure of SocialCircle paper, which is a gradual experience from perception to resonance and then to reverberation.
> In detail, SocialCircle regards that forecasting trajectories can be interpreted as *echolocation*, i.e., scanning the surrounding space to perceive others.
> *Resonance* then emphasized how agents internally and socially reflect those perceived signals, aligning their *spectrums* through mutual responses.
> Building upon these ideas, *Reverberation* turns to the final stage, modeling how such reflections persist, decay, and re-emerge as temporal echoes across time, where finally the echolocation finishes.
> Through this trilogy, the focus of our research evolves from where interactions happen (SocialCircle), to how they co-vibrate across time (*Resonance*), and finally to when and for how long their influences live (*Reverberation*), pondering the question: how perception, causality, and anticipation are intertwined.

All papers in such trilogy are built under the same primary consideration, where human intuitions and interpretations are always worthy than those limited quantitative improvements.
As authors, we have conducted as many further discussions as possibles to validate and prove such thoughts.
I have to point out that it is our great pleasure that these papers have been recognized by top-tier academic conferences and journals.
However, these discussions are mostly academic-limited.
My goal to write this post down is to shine those *untouchable* ideas and thoughts far beyond such echoes.

### Scan: SocialCircle

#### Odradek

My initial idea or motivation for crafting the SocialCircle paper is quite simple.
Back to the summer of 2023, I bought my first ever Playstation 5.
It is not my first time to play the game DEATH STRANDING.
However, I still decided to experience the process of connecting the world one more time, with more curiosity of this virtual world.
During this new playthrough, I noticed that the scanner, i.e., the odradek, appeared in the very first scene of the whole game, along with the main character Sam.

<div style="width: 60%; margin: 0 auto;">

![Odradek in Death Stranding](</assets/img/posts/2026-10-08/Screenshot 2026-10-09 at 15.33.19.png>)
*Fig. Odradek in Game Death Stranding (from THE ART OF DEATH STRANDING).*

</div>

The Odradek is originally used as the terrain scanner in the begining of the game.
Its original design shapes like a starfish, indicating its potential connections to the sea and the beach.
In the game world, it scans the front nearby fan-shaped areas by producing some kind of specific sound waves.
Holographic markers will be temporarily displayed in the world once the scanner's signal encounters different environmental objects (footprints, items, roads, or rivers) or other entities (like other deliverers, BTs, etc.).
As illustrated in the following figure, safe areas like roads will be marked as references for players to decide their future paths, and other interaction targets like cargos and entities will be also highlighted.

![Terrain Scanner and Markers](</assets/img/posts/2026-10-08/DEATH STRANDING DIRECTOR'S CUT_20230727123952.jpg>)
*Fig. Odradek as Terrain Scanner and Its Generated Markers (In-Game Capture from DEATH STRANDING DIRECTOR'S CUT).*

#### Connection

Odradek is also used by other porters or enemies.
They also use their Odradeks to scan terrains and cargos, detect any other potential risks.
During such process, no more human words or languages are needed, only those scans, just like marine animals' *echolocation*.
They use their Odradeks to scan the environment, to plan routes, to interact with others, and finally to connect the broken world.

![Porters Interact with Others Using Odradeks](</assets/img/posts/2026-10-08/DEATH STRANDING DIRECTOR'S CUT_20230727123920.jpg>)
*Fig. Porters Interact with Others Using Odradeks (In-Game Capture from DEATH STRANDING DIRECTOR'S CUT).*

Actually, the Odradek has become the direct source of the motivation of SocialCircle.
I do not know if there are any readers have found such easter egg during reading, the first figure in the SocialCircle paper just exactly describes how such Odradek functions, with almost the same holographic markers to indicate potential social interactions.
In my initial designs, it just scans, highlighting useful interaction contexts to the *porters*.
Fortunately, it works, and soon SocialCircle comes.
